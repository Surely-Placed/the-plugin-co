### 3. INDIVIDUAL SERVICE PAGES (6 pages total)
**URL Pattern:** `/services/[service-name]`
Examples:
- `/services/performance-marketing`
- `/services/seo-content-strategy`
- `/services/social-media-management`
- `/services/web-development`
- `/services/branding-design`
- `/services/ai-workflows`

**Overall Philosophy:** Each service page should convince the reader this is the right service for them RIGHT NOW, then give them confidence that we can deliver it.

**Structure for each service page:**

#### A. Hero Section
- Service name as headline (benefit-focused, not generic)
- **Examples:**
  - Not: "Performance Marketing" → But: "Performance Marketing That Drives Revenue"
  - Not: "SEO & Content Strategy" → But: "Get Found. Get Chosen. Grow."
  - Not: "Web Development" → But: "A Website That Actually Sells"
- 1-sentence value prop (the specific outcome they get)
- Hero image/graphic related to service

#### B. What We Do (Service Overview)
- Clear, non-jargon explanation of what this service actually means
- Who it's for (specific business type/stage that benefits most)
- Why it matters RIGHT NOW for their business (urgency + relevance)
- Real talk: What happens if they don't do this service
- Example framing: "Performance marketing isn't just about running ads. It's about turning every dollar into predictable revenue. If you're not doing this, you're leaving money on the table."

#### C. Our Approach (Process/Methodology)
- Headline: "How We Do It" or "Our [Service Name] Process"
- Step-by-step breakdown (4-6 steps) of how you deliver this service
- Frame it as: Discovery → Strategy → Execution → Measurement → Optimization → Ongoing Growth
- Be specific about what you actually DO (not generic consultant speak)
- This is your "secret sauce" — show confidence and specificity
- Use benefit language: "In Step 2, we identify the exact audience most likely to buy from you (not just anyone interested)"

#### D. What's Included (Service Breakdown)
- Headline: "What You Get"
- Specific, concrete deliverables (not vague promises)
- Timeline for each phase
- Key metrics you'll track and how often they report (weekly, monthly)
- Example: For Performance Marketing:
  - Campaign strategy and audience research
  - Ad creative development and A/B testing
  - Daily bid management and optimization
  - Weekly performance reports with actionable insights
  - Monthly strategy reviews and adjustments
  - Response time: 24 hours for any questions
- Frame it as: "This is what we deliver. This is what you should expect."

#### E. Results / Impact
- Headline: "What Success Looks Like"
- Be honest about realistic outcomes (not pie-in-the-sky promises)
- Typical results (e.g., "Average 2.5x ROAS in first 3 months," "80% increase in qualified leads by month 2")
- Industry examples (e.g., "For SaaS companies, we typically see...")
- **Important:** If no case studies yet, use transparent benchmarks with honest caveats
- Frame it as: "Here's what we typically see. Here's what you should expect. Here's how long it takes."

#### F. Pricing / Packages (Optional - can be hidden if not finalized)
- Starter / Standard / Premium tiers
- Or just: "Pricing starts at $X/month"
- CTA: "Request a custom proposal"

#### G. FAQ Section
- Address real objections and questions, not generic ones
- Sample questions (customize per service):
  - "How long before we see results?"
  - "What if you don't deliver on your promises?"
  - "Can you work with [specific industry/business type]?"
  - "How is this different from what we're already doing?"
  - "What's the commitment? Can we cancel anytime?"
  - "How much does this actually cost?"
- Answer tone: Honest, specific, confident. Not corporate-speak.

#### H. Related Services
- Link to 2-3 complementary services
- "Often paired with: [Service Name]"

#### I. CTA Section
- Headline: "Ready to plug in [Service Name]?"
- Subheadline: "Let's talk about what's possible for your business."
- Primary CTA: "Book a Free Consultation"
- Alternative: "Or email us your biggest challenge and we'll outline a plan"
- Frame it as a natural next step, not a hard sell

## CONTENT TONE & STYLE GUIDE

**Tone:** Direct. Confident. Young but credible. Hungry but professional. No corporate speak. No agency clichés.

**Core messaging themes to weave throughout:**
- **"Plug In"** — Integration. Seamless. No disruption. Just amplification.
- **"Amplify"** — Growth that builds on what's already working
- **"Data-driven"** — Everything is measurable. Show, don't tell. Transparency always.
- **"Full-stack"** — Comprehensive. All in one place. No vendor juggling.
- **"AI-native"** — Modern. Smart. Technology working for you, not replacing you.
- **"Results"** — Every page, every section, point back to outcomes. Not effort. Not features. Results.

**Voice guidelines:**
- Use "we" and "you" — conversational, partnering language
- Be specific. Use real numbers, real timelines, real outcomes
- Use benefit language, not feature language ("Turn your ad spend into revenue" not "We manage your ad spend")
- Use word play with the "plugin" metaphor, but don't overdo it
- Be honest about what you can and can't do
- Show personality but stay professional
- Short sentences. Punchy. Easy to scan.

**DO:**
- Use power words: "Amplify," "Accelerate," "Transform," "Drive," "Fuel"
- Use contractions ("we're," "don't," "you'll") — feels more human
- Ask rhetorical questions to engage reader
- Use metaphors that make sense (plugin, integration, amplification)
- Show confidence without arrogance
- Lead with outcomes, not process

**AVOID:**
- Generic startup buzzwords: "synergy," "leverage," "paradigm shift," "disruptive," "innovative" (unless earned)
- Corporate jargon: "best-in-class," "moving the needle," "drilling down," "take offline"
- Overused phrases: "we're passionate about," "we deliver excellence," "results-driven"
- Vague claims without proof: "amazing results" (show them), "industry-leading" (prove it)
- Wall-of-text paragraphs. Break it up. Make it skimmable.
- Stock photography language or AI-slop aesthetic
- Making promises you can't keep
